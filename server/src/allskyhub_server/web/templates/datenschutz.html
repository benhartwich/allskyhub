What data we process

Server logs. For every request the web server stores the IP address, time, requested path (without parameters), status code, amount of data and browser identifier. Purpose: operation and security of the service. Legal basis: legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The logs are deleted after 14 days.

Access request. If you ask for an account on the “Ask for access” page, we store your name, email address, how far along you are with your camera and, if you give them, your location and message. The operators receive the request by email and decide whether to invite you. Purpose: granting access. Legal basis: steps prior to entering into the user agreement at your request (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). We delete the request 30 days after the decision, unanswered ones after 180 days at the latest.

Invitation and account. Accounts exist by invitation only. For an invitation we store the email address; if you accept it, also a hash of your password (Argon2id; we do not store the password itself). Purpose: signing in and assigning your cameras to you. Legal basis: provision of the service you use (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Cameras. For paired cameras: device ID, name, camera profile, software version, latest status (e.g. exposure, temperature, free storage), data of the latest capture and the newest sky image. In addition, an archive per night: preview images about every minute and full images about every five minutes, each with capture time, exposure and sun altitude, as well as the camera's night products (keogram, star trails, time-lapse video) and sky events it detected, such as meteors, with time, measurements and image, and sky measurements (cloud cover, sky brightness, star count, star size) per capture, with statistics per night derived from them (clear hours, best sky brightness, average star size, number of meteors, auroras and thunderstorms). Purpose: display and gallery on the web and in the app. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR. The operators of allskyhub see, in an overview of all paired cameras, the name, device ID, email address of the account, camera profile, software version, state of an update and when the camera was last connected, but no images. Purpose: helping with problems and accompanying updates. Legal basis: legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Emails. We only send emails where you trigger them or have switched them on: the invitation, a link for a new password (valid for one hour, deleted afterwards), a message when a camera has been offline for several hours (if you have switched on notices about problems) and, if you have chosen “also by email”, the notifications you have switched on on the “Account” page (problems, aurora, summary of the night, clear night). They are sent through our own mail server on the same server; recipient, time and delivery status are recorded in its log.

Meteor trajectories. If two cameras see the same meteor, we calculate its trajectory (height, length, position) from both viewing directions and store it until one of the cameras is unpaired. The operators of both cameras see the result and the name of the other camera. We only match cameras of different operators if both operators have registered their camera in the camera network (camera page, “Camera network”); for this the hub calculates with the exact locations. Legal basis: your consent by switching it on (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you can withdraw at any time.

Shared cameras. If you share a camera with another account, we store this share with its role until you remove it, the other person leaves it or the camera is unpaired. The accounts it is shared with see the same images and data as you; you see their email addresses in the list of shares.

Camera map. The hub fetches map images (OpenStreetMap, Sentinel-2 cloudless by EOX, NASA Black Marble) and the place search (Nominatim) itself and caches them; for this your browser only connects to allskyhub.org, and the map services learn nothing about you. We pass search terms on to Nominatim without any link to you.

Aircraft. To show aircraft over the live image and to tell aircraft trails from meteors, the hub asks the open service adsb.lol for the aircraft within about 150 km of a point near the camera rounded to about 10 km: at night when meteor detection is on, during the day for the list “Aircraft during the day” (can be switched off per camera), and while someone is watching the live image. adsb.lol learns neither your account nor the exact location of the camera nor who is watching. We keep the aircraft positions for 15 minutes at most; for a meteor identified as an aircraft only its call sign remains. Of aircraft flying over a camera during the day we keep call sign, registration, type, time and path across the sky as long as the other data of that day; these are data broadcast publicly by the aircraft, not data about you.

Weather balloons. While someone is watching a camera's live image, the hub asks the open service SondeHub (api.v2.sondehub.org) at most once a minute for the weather balloons within about 350 km of a point rounded to 0.5° (about 55 km); all cameras in this grid share the answer. SondeHub learns neither your account nor the exact location of the camera nor who is watching. We keep the balloon positions only in memory until the next answer arrives.

Lightning satellite. To check your camera's lightning, the hub downloads the lightning data of the Meteosat weather satellite (Lightning Imager) from the Data Store of EUMETSAT, Eumetsat-Allee 1, 64295 Darmstadt, Germany, every ten minutes and keeps the lightning over Europe for 48 hours. Nothing about you or your camera goes to EUMETSAT; the hub signs in only with its own access. The comparison with the camera's location takes place on our server.

Public page. Only if you switch it on for a camera does a public page under a random link show the camera's name, its newest image with capture time, day/night and sun altitude, and the keogram and star trails of the last night. It does not show the location, device ID or other device data. It appears on the allskyhub start page (title, place and newest image) only if you switch that on as well. Switching it off invalidates the link immediately. Legal basis: your consent by switching it on (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you can withdraw at any time.

No consent is required for these (§ 165(3) TKG 2021, Austrian Telecommunications Act). No tracking, no analytics, no advertising.

Discord. Only if you enter a Discord webhook for a camera do we store its address and send the detections you choose (camera name, type, time, image) and, in the morning, the summary of the night to Discord Inc. in the USA; the data thereby leave the EU. Who sees the messages is up to you in your Discord server. Legal basis: your consent by entering it (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR); “Disconnect Discord” deletes the address.

YouTube. Only if you connect YouTube for a camera do we store the access token Google gives us for it (only for uploading videos, not for reading your channel), and every morning upload the night's time-lapse, on request in the evening also the day's, with camera name, date and the summary of the night as a video to your channel, with the visibility you chose. The recipient is Google LLC in the USA; the data thereby leave the EU. Legal basis: your consent by connecting (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR); “Disconnect YouTube” deletes the token and revokes it at Google. You manage uploaded videos on YouTube.

Plus and payment. For Plus we store per camera whether and until when Plus runs, and per account when it started the free trial month. If you buy Plus, our reseller Paddle.com Market Limited, Judd House, 18–29 Mora Street, London EC1V 8BT, United Kingdom, handles the order, payment and invoice. For this you enter your email address, country, postcode where applicable and payment details directly with Paddle; only on the purchase page does your browser load Paddle's script (cdn.paddle.com) and Paddle's checkout for this. From Paddle we only learn the identifiers of customer and subscription, status, paid period and scheduled cancellations, no payment details. For Paddle as an independent controller, its privacy policy applies; for the United Kingdom there is an adequacy decision of the European Commission. Legal basis: contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and statutory retention obligations (point (c)). We keep the subscription data as long as the subscription runs and afterwards as long as tax and company law require (7 years in Austria).

Reporting sky brightness. For a reporter account (page “Create a reporter account”) we store your email address and your password (only as a hash) and send you a confirmation link; without confirmation we delete the account after two days. Against automated sign-ups, only this page embeds Cloudflare Turnstile of Cloudflare, Inc., 101 Townsend St, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA; for this your browser connects to challenges.cloudflare.com and transmits, among other things, your IP address and browser data, also to the USA (EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework). Legal basis: our legitimate interest in protection against abuse (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). A report from the app stores the time, location (rounded to 0.1°, about 10 km), constellation, the faintest stars seen, whether clouds were in the way, and your account. On the light pollution map, reports appear without names, as the median per 10 km cell, and only once there are three reports from at least two accounts in a cell. At most one report per hour. We delete reports after one year. Legal basis: the function you requested (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). If you delete your account, we also delete your reports.

Reporting sightings. If you report a fireball or aurora in the app, we store the time, location (rounded to 0.1°, about 10 km), your details (direction in the sky, brightness, colours) and your account. We match a fireball report with the cameras' detections; others only see that there were further reports at some distance. The app shows aurora reports to everyone with time and distance, without names. We only publish an accompanying photo after we have reviewed it, with the name you give for it (or “anonymous”), under the CC BY 4.0 licence, which you agree to when uploading; before that we re-encode it so that no metadata such as location or camera remain in it. Anyone who reports a photo as inappropriate is stored with their account, so that each account reports only once. We delete sightings and photos after one year and together with your account. Legal basis: the function you requested (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR), for the photo your consent (point (a)).

Notifications nearby. If you switch on notifications for aurora, fireballs or clear nights nearby in the app, we store a place of your choice (rounded to 0.1°) and the radius for this until you switch all three off again; then we delete the place. Legal basis: your consent by switching them on (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR).

App. The app stores the hub's address and a sign-in token securely on your device. When setting up a camera, it passes the Wi-Fi name and password as well as the phone's approximate location (rounded to about 1 km, only with your permission) and its time zone directly to the camera. Wi-Fi data and location do not reach the hub in the process.

Sky in the app. Only if you open “Sky” in the app does it send the chosen place to the hub: either the phone's location (only with your permission) or a place you searched for, in each case rounded to 0.1° (about 10 km), together with the phone's time zone; if you are signed in, with your sign-in token. The app remembers the chosen place on your device. The hub uses the place only for the answer (cloud forecast, darkness, moon, aurora, passes, the nearest public camera, the sky view) and does not store it, not even in logs; we count your IP address for two days at most to limit excessive requests. For the cloud forecast the hub asks the open service Open-Meteo (Austria) for the forecast for this 0.1° cell; Open-Meteo only sees the hub, not your phone. The place search runs through the hub, as for the camera map. The app schedules calendar reminders of events only on your device; nothing is sent to us for this. The hub fetches photos of celestial objects itself once from Wikimedia Commons and stores them; Wikimedia learns nothing about you. Legal basis: the function you requested (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and, for the rate limit, our legitimate interest in a stable service (point (f)).

Storage period and deletion

We delete archived full images and time-lapse videos as well as time-lapses and keograms you created yourself after 7 days, preview images with their capture data, sky measurements, keograms and star trails after 30 days. For cameras with Plus we keep the camera's full images and time-lapse videos for 90 days, preview images with their capture data, keograms and star trails for one year; if Plus ends, the shorter periods apply again after 30 days. We delete events after 30 days (their full image after 7 days); on the “Account” page you can choose 90 days or 1 year instead, and we then keep the event and full image that long. Account and camera data as well as the statistics per night remain stored until you remove the camera or your account is deleted. If you remove a camera, we immediately delete all its images and capture data. We delete invitations that were not accepted 30 days after they expire at the latest. We delete access requests 30 days after the decision, unanswered ones after 180 days.

You can delete your account yourself at any time (“Account” page on the web or in the app), together with its cameras, images and public pages, immediately. On request we will also do this for you: support@webinx.eu.

Recipients and hosting

We do not embed any third-party services: no external fonts, scripts, CDNs or analytics tools. The only exception is the purchase page for Plus, which loads Paddle's script and checkout (see “Plus and payment”). We do not pass on any data{% if push_available %}, except for notifications you have switched on yourself (see above){% endif %}.

The server is hosted by netcup GmbH, Karlsruhe, in a data centre in Germany. netcup operates the hardware for us and technically has access to the stored data, but does not process it for its own purposes. Our servers do not transfer any data to countries outside the EU, except to Paddle when you buy Plus and to Discord and YouTube if you set them up for a camera (see above).

Die Betreiber bekommen die Anfrage per E-Mail und entscheiden, ob sie dich einladen. Zweck: Vergabe der Zugänge. Rechtsgrundlage: Anbahnung des Nutzungsvertrags auf deine Anfrage (Art. 6 Abs. 1 lit. b DSGVO). Wir löschen die Anfrage 30 Tage nach der Entscheidung, unbeantwortete spätestens nach 180 Tagen.

Einladung und Konto. Konten gibt es nur auf Einladung. Für eine Einladung speichern wir die E-Mail-Adresse; nimmst du sie an, außerdem einen Hash deines Passworts (Argon2id; das Passwort selbst speichern wir nicht). Zweck: Anmeldung und Zuordnung deiner Kameras. Rechtsgrundlage: Bereitstellung des Dienstes, den du nutzt (Art. 6 Abs. 1 lit. b DSGVO).

Kameras. Für gekoppelte Kameras: Geräte-ID, Name, Kameraprofil, Softwareversion, letzter Status (z. B. Belichtung, Temperatur, freier Speicher), Daten der letzten Aufnahme und das jeweils neueste Himmelsbild. Außerdem ein Archiv pro Nacht: Vorschaubilder etwa jede Minute und Vollbilder etwa alle fünf Minuten, jeweils mit Aufnahmezeit, Belichtung und Sonnenhöhe, sowie die Nachtprodukte der Kamera (Keogramm, Sternspuren, Zeitraffer-Video) und von ihr erkannte Himmelsereignisse wie Meteore mit Zeitpunkt, Messwerten und Bild, sowie Himmelsmesswerte (Bewölkung, Himmelshelligkeit, Sternzahl, Sterngröße) pro Aufnahme und daraus eine Statistik pro Nacht (klare Stunden, beste Himmelshelligkeit, mittlere Sterngröße, Anzahl der Meteore, Polarlichter und Gewitter). Zweck: Anzeige und Galerie im Web und in der App. Rechtsgrundlage: Art. 6 Abs. 1 lit. b DSGVO. Die Betreiber von allskyhub sehen in einer Übersicht aller gekoppelten Kameras Name, Geräte-ID, E-Mail-Adresse des Kontos, Kameraprofil, Softwareversion, Stand eines Updates und wann die Kamera zuletzt verbunden war, aber keine Bilder. E-Mails. Wir schicken E-Mails nur, wo du sie auslöst oder eingeschaltet hast: Einladung, Link für ein neues Passwort (eine Stunde gültig, danach gelöscht) eine Nachricht, wenn eine Kamera seit mehreren Stunden offline ist (wenn du Hinweise zu Problemen eingeschaltet hast) und, wenn du „auch per E-Mail“ gewählt hast, die Benachrichtigungen, die du auf der Seite „Konto“ eingeschaltet hast (Probleme, Polarlicht, Zusammenfassung der Nacht, klare Nacht). Sie gehen über unseren eigenen Mailserver auf demselben Server; Empfänger, Zeit und Zustellstatus stehen dort im Protokoll.

Meteorbahnen. Sehen zwei Kameras denselben Meteor, berechnen wir aus beiden Blickrichtungen seine Bahn (Höhe, Länge, Lage) und speichern sie, bis eine der Kameras entkoppelt wird. Die Betreiber beider Kameras sehen das Ergebnis und den Namen der anderen Kamera. Kamerakarte. Flugzeuge. Dabei geht nichts über dich oder deine Kamera an EUMETSAT; der Hub meldet sich nur mit dem eigenen Zugang an. Der Abgleich mit dem Standort der Kamera geschieht auf unserem Server.
